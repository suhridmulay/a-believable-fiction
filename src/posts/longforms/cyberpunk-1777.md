At a slight distance from the Neon, and the vibrant glowing palette of dystopia that I saw in my playthrough of Cyberpunk 2077 I was thinking of what are the core themes of Cyberpunk. The neon is beautiful, the city is by all accounts lively, the society a dystopia. Technology is flashy, and as the character creation screen reminded me, in Night City looks are everything. But a bigger question is, what lies under these looks.

Ultimately I feel Cyberpunk is a dystopia borne out of the idea of what happens when technology outgrows society's collective wisdom. For most of human history societies have remained very fluid and have adapted to technology faster and for better purposes. Cyberpunk society however is broken precisely because it never morally adapted to technology. Sure the tech in Cyberpunk is very impressive but the society surrounding it is broken beyond repair.

Residents of Night City have access to modify their body beyond their wildest dreams, yet body modification has become another area of obscene opulence. Deadly accidents no longer need to be deadly to the people of Night City, yet people will routinely be left to die if their insurance does not cover it. It is an age of amoral but perfectly legal excesses. Society has put the worth of paper deeds and words above that of people and the consequences are unashamedly out in the open. Corporations run amok with money and political power and actively harm the society.

I don't think this is a new problem however, the forces of corporate have always existed, and the honeymoon between money and government is scarcely without [historical precedent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium_Wars).

And, India, and Africa, and South East Asia, are somewhat uniquely familiar to the dangers here. Imagine the year of 1777, in India, the East India Company (EIC) 20 years ago, in 1757, installed a puppet ruler in the Bengal Subah (A Subah is like a federal province, similar to a state). About a dozen or so years ago, the Company as it was colloquially known won the right of tax collections in India, then known as Diwani. Thus, the loot of Bengal began, paving way for the subsequent looting of India. Indians were subsequently robbed of their rulers, their taxmen, and their rights. All _sold to a corporation for modest immediate political gain._

The corporation, much like a cyberpunk megacorp is quite the piece of Information Technology, while we may not immediately see it as such, the corporation represents interests across the globe, all working in tandem in a common goal. While merchant collectives have been common knowledge across Asia for generations, joint stock companies are culturally very new. The company is as much a technological shock as it's cultural. It is capable of raising vast amounts of capital and resource independently of state interests which had hitherto possesed the unique capability to do that. The traditional trading guild of the orient, was in effect outdated and out-competed. _Technology, had triumphed over an cultural and social framework established over millennia._

In Cyberpunk 2077, Night City is as much an object of the game as the player itself. The city has a way of sucking the soul out of everyone, from the overworked corporate employees, to the struggling street-people or the Klein-burgher. Night city also lacks a cohesive civic identity, the kind which established cities tend to have. In American culture, New-Yorkers are often depicted as being proud of being a part of New York city, same with Londoners in the UK. However, the same cannot be said of people of Night City, and maybe of the many many new towns that developed out of Colonial Era instability in India, Africa, and the Indies. _Did the city of Calcutta, or Bombay, have a cultural Identity in the early years of their founding?_

These are just some similarities that come to mind when playing Cyberpunk and reflecting on the history of where I am, especially in the context of what I am doing right now. Maybe there are more of these similarities. This is all the more concerning because of how I feel the world is sliding into Cyberpunk, not as a fictional dystopia, but as a playbook for the future. In that case, perhaps the message is also reassuring since while it was over 200 years of struggle. The yoke of colonialism was ultimately overthrown, the dangers of unchecked capital were understood and people were able to understand and come up with human and humane systems to achieve a better life for all. Today the reason we live in unabashed prosperity is partly the result of a better understanding, that was tragically obtained in the suffering of millions, perhaps billions of lives. It will be a lesson well learnt, if we can do it again, but with far less suffering.
